Why applying for jobs isn't enough

The application funnel is crowded and slow. Meanwhile, the real hiring conversations are already happening in public on LinkedIn. This is the missing conversation strategy.

Start Free Read the Strategy

The application funnel is crowded

When a role is posted publicly, hundreds of applicants can hit submit within hours. Your resume becomes one row in a long list, filtered by software before a human ever reads it.

Optimizing that resume still matters — it's how you win once you're in the running. But if applications are your only channel, you are competing on a very crowded field, late in the process, on terms you don't control.

LinkedIn contains live hiring conversations

Long before — and often instead of — a formal posting, hiring managers and recruiters talk in public: "We're growing the data team," "Anyone know a great PM in fintech?", "Here's what we're struggling to solve this quarter."

These are live conversations, not static job descriptions. They reveal what people are actually trying to solve right now, and they are open to anyone who shows up with something useful to say.

Thoughtful comments create visibility

Commenting beats broadcasting

Posting into the void reaches people only if you already have a large following. Commenting drops you directly into conversations that already have momentum and an engaged audience.

Relevance earns replies

A comment that references the exact post and adds real experience reads as genuinely useful. That's what gets a reply from the person doing the hiring.

Consistency compounds

Showing up thoughtfully across many relevant threads builds recognition over weeks — so the right people start to know your name before you ever apply.

Conversation precedes opportunity

Most opportunities don't start with an application. They start with a conversation: a reply that turns into a message, a message that turns into a call, a call that turns into an interview.

Applying puts you in the queue. Conversation puts you in the room. The candidates who get pulled in early are usually the ones already talking to the right people.
