## Article

### The application funnel is crowded

When a role goes public, hundreds of applicants can hit submit within hours. Your resume becomes one row in a long list, often filtered by software before a human reads it. Optimizing that resume matters&mdash;it's how you win once you're in the running&mdash;but if applications are your only channel, you're competing late, on a crowded field, on terms you don't control.

### LinkedIn contains live hiring conversations

Long before (and often instead of) a formal posting, hiring managers and recruiters talk in public: "We're growing the data team," "Anyone know a great PM in fintech?", "Here's what we're trying to solve this quarter." These are live conversations, not static job descriptions&mdash;and they're open to anyone who shows up with something useful to say.

### Thoughtful comments create visibility

Broadcasting posts into the void only works if you already have a large following. Commenting drops you into conversations that already have momentum and an engaged audience. A comment that references the exact post and adds real experience reads as genuinely useful&mdash;and that's what earns a reply from the person doing the hiring. Do it consistently and the right people start to recognize your name before you ever apply.

### Conversation precedes opportunity

Most opportunities don't start with an application. They start with a conversation: a reply becomes a message, a message becomes a call, a call becomes an interview. Applying puts you in the queue. Conversation puts you in the room.
